# Slop render-cost baseline — 2026-11-23

Pre-change baseline captured with temporary probes. The committed replacement is `tools/profile-slop.ps1`; rerun it after the frame-budget change and compare against these numbers.

Environment: PowerShell 7.6.10, .NET 10.0.14, Windows, primary display at 125% DPI. Live samples use `% of one core` from `Process.TotalProcessorTime` over 4 s windows after a warmup.

## Headless per-tick cost

Measured with `Update-SlopMotionState` at 1/120 s physics steps plus `SlopFrame.Update()` at 1/30 s and `Geometry.Parse` of the emitted path (600 iterations, 40 warmup ticks).

| Scenario | ms/tick | theoretical tick ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|
| closed compact (448x294) | 0.308 | 3,249/s |
| open compact (724x504) | 0.457 | 2,186/s |
| closed large (1884x996) | 0.349 | 2,864/s |
| open large (1884x996) | 0.550 | 1,817/s |

At 30 fps this is roughly 0.9–1.7% of a core. The much larger live figures below are WPF layout/render/composition of a layered transparent window, so the useful lever is fewer invalidating ticks, not cheaper physics.

## Live CPU samples

Three runs. The first compact sample in run 1 was contaminated by startup and is excluded from the range.

| Mode | run 1 | run 2 | run 3 | accepted range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| compact idle | (59.5 startup) | 18.7 | 11.7 | 11.7–18.7 |
| expanded idle | 39.0 | 39.0 | 48.8 | 39.0–48.8 |
| maximized expanded | 38.7 | 46.3 | 36.6 | 36.6–46.3 |

Warmup samples of 8.6% and 24.1% show how much the first seconds after launch vary; the committed harness discards them.

Interpretation: compact idle is already relatively cheap, expanded is 2–4x more expensive, and the large window adds a further modest cost while also covering the taskbar. Target outcome is a clear drop in compact and expanded idle sampling while active expand/collapse and hop behavior remain at 30 fps.
